# Nordic / Logitech / GN: Q3 2026 print previews

*29 September 2026 · Public data only · Model and sources: `README.md`; the brief answered in full: the dashboard's Analysis tab (`deliverables/my_analysis.md`)*

## Bottom line

**The view.** Each company's guide already contains the orders for the quarter it covers, so we do not try to out-forecast demand. We forecast the two things the guide leaves out: management's **usual guide error** (how far results usually land from the guide's midpoint; positive means a beat), and the state of the channel (distributors building stock, running it down, or lean).

**Is this just a spreadsheet?** For the guided quarter, yes: the arithmetic is guide × (1 + usual guide error) + known events (Nordic: 230 × 1.0523 − 1.8 = 240.2). The model decides what goes into those cells, and shows that nothing more complex does better: tested on past quarters as if in real time, models built on the supply-chain lags erred 3.9–5.9× more. The usual guide error depends on the channel state, read from distributor inventory days; the −1.8 is Logitech's supplier incident pushed through the supply graph. The chain matters one quarter later. For the quarter after the guide, the supply graph (who buys from whom, with shares and lags) erred about half as much as extrapolating the guide. That is on 9 quarters and the model was picked after seeing the test, so we treat it as suggestive. The chain also gives the direction of risk: a Q3 restocking beat would be borrowed from Q4.

**What would change it.** Distributors starting to build stock (Nordic 220.9 instead of 240.2); Logitech's lean channel refilling (upside); GN's second half not reaching the margin its full-year guide needs.

- **Nordic (22 Oct): revenue 240.2m, just above the 220–240 guide; GM 53.1%.** Since 2019 Nordic's usual guide error has been +5.23% in the 15 quarters when its distributors were neither building stock nor short of supply, which is today's state. Two of those quarters are from 2020, when end demand surged and Nordic raised its guide before the print. The channel reading cannot see a shock like that; without those two quarters the figure is +3.72%. The downside is larger than the upside: if distributors start building stock, 220.9 (−19.3). A restocking beat (254.8) would be borrowed from Q4.
- **Logitech (≈27 Oct): net sales 1,226.3m, just above the 1,185–1,220 guide; GM 45.3% against ~44% guided.** Its five quarterly guides, blended with 12 chip peers' records, give a usual guide error of +1.98%; it has beaten its GM guide 4 times out of 4. Its channel is about 2 weeks below target, with sell-through 4 points ahead of sell-in, which skews risk to the upside (sell-through plus the change in channel stock gives 1,315.7). That is not in the point, because the guide already knew that sell-through.
- **GN (5 Nov): revenue 2,247.0 DKKm (+1.6%), adj. EBITA 13.5%.** GN's August guide has been too high in 4 of 5 years (usual guide error −3.3 pts of growth), so we sit below every alternative (2,348.0–2,476.0). On the full-year guide, H2 must earn 13.5% against 11.6% bridged from Q2 (8.9% without a one-off tariff refund): either Q4 is heavy, or the guide is at risk.

The supplier incident is Logitech's (up to $200m of Oct–Dec sales), pushed through the supply graph from 25 June: −7.1m gross on Nordic's Q3 shipments, of which we assume 75% is already in the 6 Aug guide (a judgment).

**Why 240.2 and not 233.3.** Tested as if in real time over the 14 quarters since 2023, the rule behind 240.2 had a typical miss of 5.54% of the guide, no better than the bare guide midpoint (5.55%); the version blended with the peers (233.3) did best (5.06%). We keep 240.2. Fourteen quarters are too few to choose a rule by its score, so we change a rule only when we have a reason from the business. Blending assumes Nordic guides like its peers, and it does not: its usual guide error is +5.23% against their +2.02%, and the blended version under-predicted Nordic in every quarter of 2025–26. One choice was made after seeing the data: quarters of chip shortage are kept apart from lean ones, because in a shortage Nordic's revenue was set by wafer supply. That split does not improve the test either (the rule before it gives 238.0). 233.3 and 238.0 are logged now and scored against the print on 22 Oct.

## The supply-chain decisions behind the call

**1. Lag: a change in Amazon sell-out reaches Nordic's revenue after about 24 weeks.** Reasoned edge by edge before any regression: Amazon cuts orders to Logitech (~4.5 weeks including weekly replenishment), then Logitech's builds and chip orders reach Nordic (~19.6 weeks including Logitech's monthly planning, the ODMs' material planning and distributor reorders). The order signal takes ≈24 weeks (90% range 21–27; ≈1.8 quarters); the goods alone take ≈17. Planning delays are not disclosed; if part of them already sits in inventory cover, the Amazon route is 19–24 weeks. The data fit anywhere from 19.5 to 37 weeks (one cycle). The lag moves with the cycle: ~4 quarters in the 2021–22 shortage, ~2 in the destock, each from one turning point. *In the forecast:* chip lead times of 12–16 weeks mean Q3's demand was ordered before Nordic guided, so the chain gets no weight in the guided quarter; the graph drives Q4.

**2. Mechanism: the channel's stock moves revenue; the companies' own inventories do not.** Revenue is sell-in; the gap to sell-through is the change in channel stock. Nordic's own 193 inventory days (a build management attributes to nRF54 wafers; not quote-checked) is not a demand signal. Only Logitech quantifies its channel: its sell-through gap, anchored on its "at target" statements (±0.7 weeks), shows about 2 weeks lean today. Nordic describes its distributors only in words (healthy; some small customers may order ahead). GN Enterprise's sell-out has matched or beaten sell-in for 8 quarters. The swing is amplified at the chip tier: Nordic's consumer revenue moves 2.7× as much as Logitech's sell-in. *In the forecast:* the channel enters once, as its state in the quarter before the guide: when distributors were building stock, the peers' guide error fell by 1.64 pts (619 peer quarters, statistically significant) and Nordic's by 8.39.

**3. Attribution: Logitech + GN ≈ 16% of Nordic's revenue (13–20%), ≈ 27% of Nordic Consumer.** Nordic names no customers, so five independent estimates bracket the share: a bottom-up count from FCC teardown photos (units × radios per unit × Nordic's share of sockets × chip price); a cap if Nordic invoices Logitech directly (it discloses no customer above 10% of revenue except two distributors); the PC-peripheral share of Nordic's revenue; a floor from Nordic's proprietary-radio revenue; and the 2022–24 downturn, when Nordic Consumer fell far more than Logitech could explain. The 16% assumes Logitech buys through ODMs; ≈ 11% if Nordic invoices it directly. GN is ≈ $8m. *In the forecast:* no weight in the guided quarter, so the share does not move the point. One quarter out Nordic moves ≈ 3× what the share implies, because its other consumer customers ride the same cycle.

**4. Structural breaks: the shortage broke the Nordic–Logitech link; the customer mix and the lags held.** Each event was tested before and after its date. The 2021–22 shortage: the link breaks at its end (2022Q4, statistically significant); those quarters are excluded from the lag fits. The 2022–24 distributor build and destock: in 2023 Nordic's top-10 customers fell 3%, its broad market 46%. The 2025 US price step (2.8% of Logitech's sales) means dollars stop tracking units; three quarters cannot test the link. Post-COVID normalisation moved the input, not the link. The AI / memory cycle breaks only the proxies, and not significantly. Logitech's 2026 supplier incident is a one-off shock, not a break: it removes Logitech builds for one or two quarters, and nothing suggests it changes the lags or route shares. One quarter cannot test that; a lasting re-sourcing would show first in Logitech's new FCC grants (Monitor tab). It enters as an event term: −1.8m on Q3 after the 75% assumed in the guide, −0.9m on Q4. While it lasts, Logitech's sales understate Nordic's wider consumer cycle. Our Q4 model had counted the incident twice; fixing that on 29 Sep moved Q4 from 208.9 to 212.0. Both numbers are logged and will be scored.

**5. Not built: no Amazon rank, price or promotion scrapes.** There is no free history to test them (Keepa is paid). Existing sources and libraries instead: SEC XBRL, ECB rates, findchips, FCC filings. Deliberately stopped: the FCC count at 38 of 81 public grants, the factor search after about a dozen candidates.

**6. Limitations: for the guided quarter nothing beats the guide.** Lag models err 3.9–5.9× more than guide + usual guide error, and across 12 peers the beat varies only 3.9% against 29% for revenue growth: the beat is management's forecast error, not demand. Weakest links: the channel's *level* (nobody discloses weeks on hand), the invoicing route behind attribution, and a channel reading whose thresholds were set after seeing 2020–26, which flags turns a quarter late and cannot see an end-demand shock (the 2020 surge quarters sit in the usual guide error). The chain does not help GN (~1% of Nordic).
